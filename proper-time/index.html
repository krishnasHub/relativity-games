Missions

The physics

You fly a relativistic courier with a perfect antimatter drive. Everything you see from the cockpit is computed from special relativity, frame by frame.

Exact

  • Rapidity. Thrust adds rapidity φ, not speed. Speed is β = tanh φ, so it creeps toward c and never gets there. γ = cosh φ.
  • Time dilation. Your clock is the real one you feel (1 s of play = 1 s of ship time). The universe's clock runs γ times faster.
  • Length contraction. Distances along your motion shrink by γ in your frame. That is why a 2,400 light-second trip can take you 20 seconds.
  • Aberration. Light reaching you is tilted toward your direction of travel: tan(θ′/2) = e−φ tan(θ/2). The sky crowds into a bright ring ahead (the "starbow"), and things you have passed can still appear ahead of you.
  • Doppler shift. Light from direction θ is shifted by D = γ(1 + β cos θ). Stars are blackbodies, and a blackbody at temperature T looks like one at D·T, so colours are computed from the real Planck spectrum and the CIE 1931 colour-matching functions.
  • Beaming. A point source gets D² brighter and sky surface brightness D⁴. At γ ≈ 600 the 2.7 K cosmic microwave background is blueshifted into visible light.
  • Light delay. Blinking beacons and spinning planets are shown as they were when their light left them, so approaching clocks run fast and receding ones slow.
  • Rocket equation. A photon rocket gains rapidity Δφ = ln(m₀/m₁). Each extra "nine" of c costs the same 1.15 of rapidity.

Tuned so it plays (sci-fi)

  • Space is compressed. Stars are light-seconds apart, not light-years, so trips take seconds instead of years. Relativity is the same at any scale.
  • Inertial dampers. Full thrust is about 30 million g. Your crew is fine. Don't ask.
  • A perfect drive. Antimatter converts all its mass to thrust.
  • Brightness is compressed. Nearby stars and planets are drawn about 10¹⁰ times dimmer than reality so you can see them next to the starfield, and your eyes auto-adjust exposure (lock it with X to see the raw change). Colour shifts and brightness changes stay exact.
  • Small things: planet surface colours are tinted after the Doppler shift instead of being simulated by wavelength, and the ship's axes ignore Thomas precession.

Press V in flight to switch to a Newtonian view: what an observer would see with no relativity. That is never what you'd actually see.

Controls

Click the view to capture the mouse (or drag to look). Keyboard and mouse needed.

  • Mouse or ↑↓←→ look and turn the ship. QE roll. Wheel zoom.
  • W thrust where you are facing. Hold Shift for fine thrust.
  • S brake against your motion, whichever way you face. Near your target the arrival computer throttles the burn so you stop at it.
  • Space reverse thrust: full power backwards from where you are facing.
  • T face the target. F face where you are going. R look behind. Z zoom in on the starbow (and back out).
  • V relativistic or Newtonian view. M spacetime diagram. X lock exposure. L lock on to a beacon. H hide the HUD.
  • Esc pause.
